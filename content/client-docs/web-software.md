Software development can involve substantial technical expertise, complex architecture and significant development expenditure.  However, not all software development qualifies for R&D tax relief.  The critical distinction is whether the work simply delivers new or improved commercial functionality, or seeks an advance in the wider field of technology by addressing genuine technological uncertainties that a competent professional could not readily resolve.

What types of R&D activities qualify?

Potentially qualifying activities may arise in:

- SaaS and enterprise platforms: New or significantly enhanced approaches to distributed architecture, scalability, concurrency and system resilience.

- Artificial intelligence and machine learning: Technological advances in model development, algorithms, computational methods and reliability.

- Fintech and payments technology: Technological advances in high-volume transaction processing, real-time ledgers, reconciliation, idempotency, fraud detection, security, auditability and resilient integration with regulated or legacy systems.

- Data engineering: Development of new methods for processing, synchronising or analysing complex and high-volume data.

- Cloud technologies: Advances involving fault tolerance, resource management, performance and distributed processing.

- Cybersecurity: Investigation of new technical methods to address security vulnerabilities or technological limitations.

Eligibility depends on the specific technological advances and uncertainties, not simply the use of sophisticated technology.

Common R&D Tax Questions for Software, SaaS and FinTech Companies

1. What software development qualifies for R&D tax relief?

The challenge

Software businesses frequently develop new platforms, features, integrations and applications. Although these projects can be commercially innovative, HMRC requires evidence of an advance in the wider field of technology and the associated technological uncertainties faced.

A company may have invested heavily in developing a system without undertaking qualifying R&D.

How iTax can help

We examine the technical objectives, existing technologies, limitations of established approaches and the development work undertaken.

By engaging with software architects and competent professionals, we identify technological uncertainties that could not readily be resolved and distinguish qualifying work from routine software development.  We also explain where projects do not meet the R&D definition, providing a more reliable basis for making a claim.

2. What evidence does HMRC expect for a software R&D claim?

The challenge

Software teams often maintain extensive development records, but these may be spread across project management systems, code repositories, technical documentation and internal communications.

Tickets frequently explain what was developed without recording why an established technological approach was inadequate or what uncertainty engineers were trying to resolve.

How iTax can help

We work with technical leads to review relevant development records, architecture decisions, testing evidence and technical investigations.  We then work with them to develop a structured account of the technological baseline, intended advance, uncertainties and work undertaken.

Our evidence led process ensures the R&D activities are correctly captured.

3. Does AI or machine learning development qualify for R&D tax relief?

The challenge

AI and machine learning projects can involve extensive experimentation, but companies may be uncertain whether their work represents genuine technological development or the application of existing tools.

Using generative AI, integrating large language models or improving a product through AI does not automatically satisfy HMRC's definition.

How iTax can help

We assess whether the development addressed technological uncertainties beyond existing tools, algorithms and frameworks.

Where relevant, we examine performance, reliability, scalability, architecture and other technological limitations.

We focus on demonstrable technological advances, not commercial novelty.

4. How do we calculate qualifying developers’ time and costs?

The challenge

Developers frequently work across R&D, feature development, bug fixing, support and maintenance.

Companies may not maintain timesheets separating these activities, making it difficult to support staff cost allocations.

How iTax can help

We review available development records, project timelines, team responsibilities and relevant financial information.

We develop a reasonable, documented allocation methodology that reflects the actual qualifying work undertaken.

5. Can we claim cloud computing, software and data licence costs?

Potentially. Revenue expenditure on software, cloud computing services and data licences may qualify where it is attributable to activities that directly contribute to resolving the technological uncertainty. Mixed-use costs need a reasonable, evidence-based apportionment, and specific exclusions can apply.

6. Can outsourced software development or contractor costs qualify?

Potentially, but the answer depends on the contractual arrangements, who decided or intended that the R&D would be undertaken, and where the activity was carried out. Contracts, statements of work and the surrounding facts should be reviewed before costs are included or the right to claim is assumed.

7. Can we claim for developers or contractors based overseas?

Overseas contractor and externally provided worker costs are restricted for accounting periods beginning on or after 1 April 2024, subject to limited statutory exceptions. The location of the R&D activity and the precise engagement model need to be established rather than inferred from where the supplier is incorporated.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does developing a new SaaS platform qualify for R&D tax relief?

Not automatically.

Developing a new SaaS platform may qualify where the project seeks an advance in technology and involves uncertainties that competent professionals could not readily resolve.

However, developing features, interfaces and functionality using established software techniques would not ordinarily constitute qualifying R&D.

Can AI and machine learning development qualify?

Yes, where the development seeks a qualifying advance in science or technology.

Using existing AI models, libraries or APIs does not automatically qualify. The assessment must identify the underlying scientific or technological uncertainties and the work undertaken to resolve them.

Can complex systems integration qualify as R&D?

Potentially.

System uncertainty can qualify where it is not readily deducible how existing technologies or components can be combined to achieve the intended technological capability.

Routine integration using established approaches would not generally qualify.

Does routine debugging qualify?

Routine debugging, maintenance and fixing ordinary software defects do not normally qualify.

However, technical investigation undertaken to resolve a qualifying technological uncertainty may form part of R&D, even where it involves diagnosing unexpected system behaviour.

Can unsuccessful software development projects qualify?

Yes.

A project may qualify even where the intended technological advance was not achieved, provided the work genuinely sought to resolve qualifying technological uncertainty.

What evidence should we retain for software R&D?

Useful evidence includes architecture documentation, technical specifications, development tickets, code history, performance tests, experimental results and technical decision records.

The evidence should help establish why the uncertainty existed and how the company attempted to resolve it.
