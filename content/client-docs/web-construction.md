# R&D Tax Relief for Construction and the Built Environment

Specialist advice for construction businesses undertaking genuine engineering or technological innovation.

Construction and civil engineering projects often involve demanding specifications, difficult site conditions and specialist engineering solutions. Yet complexity, novelty or a bespoke design does not, by itself, make a project eligible for R&D tax relief.

Eligibility depends on whether the work sought an advance in science or technology by resolving uncertainty that a competent professional could not readily resolve using publicly available knowledge. Under the merged R&D scheme, businesses must also establish who is entitled to claim when work is carried out for a customer or through subcontractors.

## Where R&D May Arise in Construction

Potentially qualifying activity may arise where established knowledge or techniques cannot readily deliver the required outcome, for example in:

- Civil and structural engineering: Technological development involving structural methods, performance or resilience.

- Flood resilience: Development of new technological approaches to flood mitigation, drainage or resilient structures.

- Construction materials: Investigation of new materials, formulations and performance characteristics.

- Ground engineering: Development of new technical methods to overcome geological or geotechnical limitations.

- Sustainable construction: Technological advances involving materials, energy systems or environmental performance.

- Specialist construction methods: Development of new approaches to engineering, fabrication or installation where existing techniques are insufficient.

What usually falls outside the relief: routine design, ordinary site adaptation, regulatory compliance, aesthetic or commercial innovation, and the straightforward application of established construction methods.

## Common R&D Tax Challenges for Construction Companies

### 1. We Developed the Solution — but Are We Entitled to Claim?

#### The challenge

Construction projects commonly involve developers, main contractors, specialist subcontractors and external consultants. The business carrying out the technical work is not necessarily the business entitled to claim. The key question is whether the relevant R&D was intended or contemplated by the customer when the work was contracted out.

#### How iTax Advisors helps

We review contracts, scope, specifications and the circumstances in which the technical uncertainty arose. We then apply the contracted-out R&D rules to establish a supportable claim position before detailed expenditure calculations begin.

### 2. Our Projects Involve Complex Engineering — Is That Enough?

#### The challenge

Difficult ground conditions, unusual specifications and bespoke designs can demand considerable expertise. However, a project may still rely on established engineering principles. A valid claim must identify the wider technological advance sought and the uncertainty that could not readily be resolved by a competent professional.

#### How iTax Advisors helps

We work with engineers and other competent professionals to define the technological baseline, explain why established solutions were insufficient and map the investigations, testing and iterations used to resolve the uncertainty.

### 3. Most of Our Costs Relate to Subcontractors and Site Labour

#### The challenge

Projects often use employees, agency workers, self-employed tradespeople and specialist subcontractors. Much of that expenditure may relate to ordinary construction rather than R&D, and each labour arrangement must be analysed under the correct statutory category. Overseas subcontractor and externally provided worker costs are also restricted under the merged scheme, subject to narrow exceptions.

#### How iTax Advisors helps

We review the contractual basis of the spend and the activities performed. We separate ordinary delivery costs from qualifying R&D and assess the treatment of staffing costs, externally provided workers and contracted-out R&D, including any overseas restrictions.

### 4. Our Technical Evidence Is Spread Across the Project Team

#### The challenge

Evidence may sit across architects, engineers, environmental specialists, project managers and subcontractors. Records often describe what was built, but not the technological baseline, the uncertainty encountered, the alternatives considered or the point at which R&D began and ended.

#### How iTax Advisors helps

We bring together proportionate, contemporaneous support such as design revisions, calculations, technical reports, test results, meeting notes and project correspondence. We also document the competent professional’s reasoning so the claim explains not only what changed, but why the work met the statutory R&D test.

### 5. We Are Unsure About Claim Deadlines and HMRC Requirements

#### The challenge

First-time or returning claimants may need to submit a claim notification within six months of the end of the relevant accounting period. Every claim must also be supported by an Additional Information Form. Missed deadlines, weak project descriptions or cost schedules that do not reconcile to the technical work can prevent or undermine an otherwise valid claim.

#### How iTax Advisors helps

We confirm the relevant scheme, notification position and filing timetable at the outset. We then align the technical narrative, expenditure analysis and statutory disclosures so the submission is complete, consistent and ready for review.

## Frequently Asked Questions

Can construction companies claim R&D tax relief?

Yes, where qualifying scientific or technological R&D is undertaken and the company satisfies the relevant claim conditions.

However, complex construction projects, bespoke designs and difficult site conditions do not automatically qualify.

Can a subcontractor claim R&D tax relief?

Potentially.

Under the merged scheme, the entitlement to claim depends on the contracted-out R&D rules, including whether the customer intended or contemplated the relevant R&D.

Where the customer contracted out the R&D, the customer will generally be entitled to claim, subject to applicable exceptions. A contractor may qualify where the R&D was not contracted out to it and the relevant conditions are satisfied.

Can construction companies claim for site labour?

Only where the activities relate to eligible R&D activities and expenditures satisfy the statutory rules.

Ordinary excavation, installation, assembly and other construction work do not qualify merely because they take place on a project involving R&D.  They must be linked to the R&D activities.

Staff costs externally provided workers and contractor payments must be assessed under their respective rules.

Does sustainable construction qualify for R&D tax relief?

Potentially.

Developing new/enhanced construction technologies, materials or engineering methods may qualify where the work seeks a scientific or technological advance.

Simply installing established sustainable products or meeting environmental standards does not automatically qualify.

Can unusual ground conditions or flood risks lead to qualifying R&D?

Yes, potentially, but difficult site conditions alone are not sufficient.

There must be an advance sought in overall science or technology, with qualifying uncertainty that could not readily be resolved using established engineering knowledge.

Does obtaining planning permission or regulatory approval establish R&D eligibility?

No.

Planning approval, building regulations compliance and other regulatory decisions do not themselves establish scientific or technological advancement.

Eligibility depends on the underlying technological work undertaken.
