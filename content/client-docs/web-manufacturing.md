Web page - R&D tax relief for manufacturing companies

Identifying and supporting qualifying R&D in product development, materials, machinery and manufacturing processes.

Manufacturing businesses may undertake qualifying R&D when developing products, investigating materials, adapting machinery or processes, or attempting to overcome scientific or technological limitations. The key question is not whether the work was innovative or commercially valuable, but whether it sought an advance in science or technology and involved uncertainty that a competent professional could not readily resolve.

Some of this work may qualify for R&D tax relief, but qualifying activities are frequently embedded within wider production and engineering operations.

What manufacturing activities can qualify for R&D tax relief?

Qualifying R&D may arise in:

- New product development: Development of products with new or appreciably improved scientific or technological capabilities.

- Advanced materials: Investigation of material properties, formulations, composition and performance.

- Manufacturing processes: Development of new or enhanced methods to overcome technological limitations in production.

- Machinery and equipment: Technological development involving specialist systems, machinery and production equipment.

- Prototypes and experimental production: Design, testing and technical evaluation aimed at resolving uncertainty.

- Waste and resource recovery: Development of new technological methods for recycling, processing and reusing materials.

Routine product changes, production optimisation and the adoption of established manufacturing processes do not qualify.

Common R&D tax challenges for manufacturers

1. We undertake production trials — are these R&D?

The challenge

Manufacturers frequently undertake production trials involving new raw materials, processing parameters, machinery configurations and operating methods.

However, a trial does not necessarily qualify merely because the outcome was uncertain.

The distinction between genuine technological uncertainty and normal production experimentation is critical.

How iTax can help

We review the technological objectives, existing capabilities and technical issues encountered.

We examine trial records, testing results and development decisions to establish whether the work sought an advance in technology that competent professionals could not readily achieve.

2. Our R&D is embedded within everyday manufacturing operations

The challenge

Qualifying work may be undertaken by production engineers, technical managers, process specialists and other personnel as part of wider operational responsibilities.  It may not be recorded separately or recognised as R&D for tax purposes.  As a result, companies may overlook qualifying activities or struggle to separate them from routine operations.

How iTax can help

We undertake structured discussions with relevant technical and operational personnel to identify potentially qualifying projects across the business.

We review technical developments beyond formal research departments, helping establish the scope of qualifying activities and the personnel involved.

3. How do we identify eligible materials and consumables?

The challenge

Experimental production and testing can involve substantial expenditure on raw materials, formulations, components and prototypes.

Not all materials used during development will necessarily qualify, particularly where activities overlap with commercial production.

How iTax can help

We examine materials usage, project records and financial information to determine the appropriate treatment of potentially eligible consumables.

We establish which costs relate to qualifying R&D and develop an evidence-based calculation that distinguishes experimental work from ordinary production.

4. We have improved an existing process — is that enough?

The challenge

Manufacturers may improve yield, quality, consistency, waste levels or production efficiency by changing an existing process. These commercial outcomes do not determine eligibility: the relevant question is whether achieving them required an advance in overall scientific or technological capability and the resolution of uncertainty that could not readily be addressed using established techniques.

However, improvements arising from established techniques may not constitute qualifying R&D.

How iTax can help

We consider whether the work sought a genuine technological advance and whether the improvement required investigation beyond routine engineering or process optimisation.

We document the relevant technical baseline, limitations, experimental work and results.

Frequently asked questions

Which R&D tax relief scheme applies to manufacturers? For accounting periods beginning on or after 1 April 2024, most companies claim under the merged R&D expenditure credit scheme. A separate enhanced scheme may be available to loss-making, R&D-intensive SMEs. The appropriate treatment depends on the company’s circumstances and accounting period.

Does improving an existing manufacturing process qualify?

Potentially.

A process improvement may qualify where it involves seeking an advance in overall technological capability and resolving uncertainty that competent professionals could not readily overcome.

Routine improvements using established techniques would not normally qualify.

Can unsuccessful production trials qualify?

Yes.

Work undertaken to resolve qualifying technological uncertainty can constitute R&D even where the experiments fail or the intended improvement is not achieved.

Can we claim the cost of subcontracted R&D? Potentially. Under the rules for accounting periods beginning on or after 1 April 2024, entitlement depends in part on which company intended or contemplated that R&D would be undertaken. The contractual arrangements, scope of work and location of the activities should be reviewed before subcontractor costs are included in a claim.

Are raw materials used in R&D trials eligible?

Potentially. Revenue expenditure on materials, components, water, fuel and power may qualify to the extent that the items are consumed or transformed directly in qualifying R&D. A reasonable apportionment may be needed where they are also used in routine production. Where the R&D results in an item that is sold or otherwise transferred at arm’s length, the cost of consumables incorporated into that item cannot be claimed.

Can production engineers' salaries qualify?

Yes, to the extent that their eligible staff costs relate to qualifying R&D activities or qualifying indirect activities.

Where personnel undertake a mixture of R&D and other responsibilities, the allocation should be reasonable and supportable.

Can overseas engineering, testing or subcontractor costs qualify? Costs relating to overseas contractors and externally provided workers are generally restricted for accounting periods beginning on or after 1 April 2024. A limited exception may apply where conditions necessary for the R&D are not present in the UK, are present overseas and it would be wholly unreasonable to replicate them in the UK. Lower cost or the availability of overseas workers is not sufficient by itself.

Does purchasing new machinery qualify for R&D tax relief?

The capital cost of purchasing machinery does not, by itself, qualify as R&D expenditure under the revenue-based R&D tax relief schemes. However, staff, consumables, software, contractor or other eligible revenue costs incurred in developing, adapting or testing machinery may qualify where the activities form part of a project seeking a scientific or technological advance. R&D Allowances may also be relevant where qualifying capital expenditure is incurred on facilities or equipment used for R&D.

Where the machinery is used in qualifying research, other tax reliefs, including R&D Allowances (accelerated capital allowances), may be relevant.

Qualifying development activities associated with new machinery should be considered separately from the capital cost of acquiring it.

Can we claim for R&D undertaken on an operational production line?

Yes, potentially.

Qualifying research may take place within an operational manufacturing environment, provided the relevant activities directly contribute to resolving scientific or technological uncertainty.

Routine production expenditure must be distinguished from qualifying experimental work.

What must we submit to make an R&D tax relief claim? Every claim requires an Additional Information Form before, or on the same day as, the Company Tax Return is submitted. Some companies must also submit a claim notification form within the applicable time limit. Eligibility for the underlying R&D does not remove these procedural requirements, so the filing position should be checked early.
