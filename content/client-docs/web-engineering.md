Which engineering projects qualify for R&D tax relief, what costs can be claimed, and how can you build a robust, evidence-led claim?

Engineering businesses regularly develop new or improved products, processes, materials and systems. Projects may involve demanding tolerances, performance requirements, system integration, scale-up or operating conditions.  However, technical difficulty, bespoke design and innovation do not automatically make the work eligible for R&D tax relief.

Eligibility depends on whether the work seeks an advance in the wider field of science or technology and involves uncertainties that competent professionals could not readily resolve.

Engineering Activities That May Qualify for R&D Tax Relief

Potentially eligible activities can arise where engineers seek an advance in overall scientific or technological knowledge or capability and must resolve uncertainties that are not readily deducible by a competent professional. High-level examples include:

- Mechanical engineering: Development of new mechanical systems, components or performance capabilities.

- Electrical and electronic engineering: Technological advances in electronic systems, power management, controls and integration.

- Civil and structural engineering: Development of new engineering methods, materials or structural technologies.

- Environmental engineering: Technological development involving water treatment, waste processing, pollution management or resource recovery.

- Materials engineering: Investigation of new material properties and technical performance characteristics.

- Product development: Experimental design, prototyping and technical validation aimed at resolving technological uncertainty.

- Systems engineering: Development involving complex interactions between components or processes where the technological solution is not readily deducible.

The relevant question is not simply whether a project was new, complex or required experienced engineers, but whether the work sought a qualifying technological advance and involved scientific or technological uncertainties that competent professionals could not readily resolve.

Common R&D Tax Challenges for Engineering Businesses

1. Our Projects Are Technically Difficult — Does That Mean They Qualify?

The challenge

Engineering businesses regularly encounter complex client requirements, difficult design specifications and demanding operating conditions.

A project may require substantial technical expertise without necessarily involving an advance in the wider field of technology.

How iTax can help

We establish the technological baseline at the outset of the project: what was already publicly available or readily deducible, which established engineering methods could be used, and where those methods were inadequate. We then identify the specific advance sought and the uncertainties that could not readily be resolved.

We work with relevant competent professionals to distinguish qualifying uncertainty from routine design, adaptation and implementation.

2. When Does Engineering R&D Begin and End?

The challenge

Engineering projects can progress through feasibility studies, design, simulation, prototyping, testing, validation and production.  Not all these activities necessarily qualify.

Companies sometimes struggle to establish the point at which qualifying R&D began and when the relevant technological uncertainties were resolved, resulting in either overclaiming or underclaiming.

How iTax can help

We interview the technical leads to reconstruct the phases the project has been through and identify when the “R&D clock” started, when work began to resolve a qualifying scientific or technological uncertainty and when it stopped because the uncertainty was resolved or the work ceased.

We support that assessment by examining development timelines, design revisions, calculations, simulations, technical investigations, prototype and test records, failures and key decision points. This helps separate qualifying work from initial scoping, routine design, commercial production and post-resolution validation.

3. Can We Claim the Costs of Prototypes and Testing?

The challenge

Prototype development may involve engineering personnel, materials, specialist testing, software, external contractors and capital equipment.

Distinguishing qualifying development expenditure from capital investment, routine verification and commercial production can be complex.

How iTax can help

We review the purpose of prototype development, the technical uncertainties involved and the nature of the expenditure.

We then identify potentially qualifying cost categories and develop a documented financial analysis.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does bespoke engineering design qualify for R&D tax relief?

Not automatically.

A bespoke solution may involve considerable engineering expertise without advancing overall technological knowledge or capability.

Eligibility depends on whether the work seeks a scientific or technological advance and involves qualifying uncertainty.

Can prototype development qualify?

Yes, where the prototype work directly contributes to resolving qualifying scientific or technological uncertainty.

Routine product demonstrations, commercial prototypes and ordinary validation following resolution of the uncertainty do not automatically qualify.

Can CAD modelling and engineering simulations qualify?

Potentially.

Modelling and simulation used to investigate qualifying technological uncertainty may form part of R&D.

Routine design calculations or verification using established methods would not ordinarily qualify.

Can unsuccessful engineering projects qualify?

Yes.

R&D does not require the successful development of a final product or solution.

What matters is whether the company undertook activities directed at resolving genuine scientific or technological uncertainty.

Can engineering consultants and subcontractors' costs qualify?

Potentially, depending on the contractual arrangements, nature of the work, where it is performed and the relevant expenditure rules.

The entitlement to claim contracted-out R&D must be established before including the associated costs.

Can R&D continue after a prototype has been developed?

Potentially, where new qualifying scientific or technological uncertainties emerge that require further investigation.

However, routine validation, production preparation and ordinary fault rectification would not generally qualify merely because they follow a development project.

What engineering R&D costs can be claimed?

Potentially qualifying categories can include staffing costs, externally provided workers, consumable items, software, data licences, cloud computing and payments to contractors, to the extent that they relate to qualifying R&D activities. The treatment depends on the facts, the accounting period and the relevant scheme.  Capital expenditure, routine production and ordinary commercial costs are not automatically eligible.

Who can claim where engineering R&D is carried out under a customer contract?

For accounting periods beginning on or after 1 April 2024, entitlement may depend on who decided or intended that the R&D should be undertaken, viewed in light of the contract and surrounding circumstances. Engineering businesses should assess claim ownership before including work commissioned by a customer or activities passed to a contractor.

Can overseas engineering contractors or externally provided workers qualify?

Overseas contractor and externally provided worker costs are generally restricted for accounting periods beginning on or after 1 April 2024. A narrow exception may apply where conditions necessary for the R&D are not present in the UK and it would be wholly unreasonable to replicate them here; lower cost or a shortage of UK workers is not sufficient on its own.

What records should an engineering company keep to support an R&D claim?

Useful contemporaneous evidence may include design briefs, technical specifications, calculations, CAD revisions, simulation outputs, test plans and results, failure logs, meeting notes, emails, timesheets and cost records. The evidence should show the starting knowledge, the advance sought, the uncertainties encountered, the work undertaken and how costs were attributed.

When does an engineering company need to notify HMRC of an R&D claim?

Some companies must submit a claim notification within six months of the end of the relevant period of account. An Additional Information Form is also required before or on the same day as the Company Tax Return containing the claim. The filing position should be checked early, particularly for first-time claimants or businesses returning to R&D relief after a gap.
