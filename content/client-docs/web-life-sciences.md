R&D Tax Relief Across the Life Sciences Sector

Qualifying R&D may arise across the life sciences development pathway, from early discovery and laboratory research through clinical development, diagnostics, medical technology and bioprocess scale-up.

- Drug discovery and therapeutics: Investigation of new therapeutic compounds, biological mechanisms, formulations and treatment approaches.

- Biotechnology and vaccine development: Research into biological systems, vaccine technologies, immunological responses and manufacturing processes.

- Clinical research: Scientific investigations and qualifying development activities undertaken during clinical programmes.

- Medical devices and diagnostics: Development of new or significantly enhanced technologies, diagnostic methods, monitoring systems and medical devices.

- Bioprocess development: Overcoming scientific or technological uncertainties involving biological production, purification, scale-up and reproducibility.

- Digital health technologies: Development involving genuine technological advances in medical software, data analysis or integrated diagnostic systems.

Not all activities within these programmes will necessarily qualify. The assessment must identify the advances sought in the wider field of science or technology and the work undertaken to resolve associated uncertainties.

Common R&D Tax Challenges for Life Sciences Companies

Life sciences businesses are operating in a selective funding environment, with longer development timelines, pressure on cash runway and greater investor scrutiny of technical and financial discipline. Against that backdrop, changes to the R&D tax regime have made the timing, value and defensibility of claims more important—and more complex.

The issues most frequently affecting biotechnology, pharmaceutical, MedTech and diagnostics companies now include:

1. Will Our Overseas Clinical Trials, CRO and Specialist Laboratory Costs Still Qualify?

The challenge

Life sciences companies frequently conduct research internationally because of clinical trial recruitment requirements, specialist facilities, manufacturing capabilities and regulatory considerations.

For accounting periods beginning on or after 1 April 2024, restrictions on overseas contractor and externally provided worker costs can substantially reduce claims. The position is particularly difficult where patient populations, specialist facilities, regulatory requirements or scientific conditions mean work must take place outside the UK.

2. Who Is Entitled to Claim When R&D Is Delivered Through CROs, CDMOs or Research Partners?

The challenge

Under the merged scheme, claim entitlement can depend on whether the customer intended or contemplated R&D of that sort when the contract was entered into. CRO and CDMO agreements may not clearly record who initiated the R&D, controlled the scientific programme, bore the risk or expected to claim the relief.

This can create uncertainty for both sponsors and service providers, particularly where scopes evolve, work packages overlap or a contractor undertakes its own qualifying R&D beyond the customer’s contemplated programme.

3. Can We Evidence the Claim Without Creating an Additional Burden for Scientists?

The challenge

Life sciences businesses typically maintain laboratory records, research protocols, experimental results and scientific reports.

However, those records are usually prepared for scientific, regulatory or operational purposes. They may not clearly set out the baseline in the relevant field, the advance sought, why the uncertainties were not readily deducible, or when qualifying R&D began and ended.

The Additional Information Form and increased HMRC scrutiny also require better coordination between scientific and finance teams, often when internal resources are already stretched.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS

Can clinical trial expenditure qualify for R&D tax relief?

Yes. Activities undertaken during clinical development may qualify where they directly contribute to resolving scientific or technological uncertainty.

However, not every clinical trial activity or associated cost is automatically eligible. The scientific purpose, nature of the expenditure and applicable statutory rules must be assessed.

For payments to clinical trial subjects to qualify, they must be made to the subjects themselves in return for participating in the trial, rather than being paid through a regulatory body or other intermediary.

Can we claim expenditure incurred through overseas CROs?

Potentially. Overseas contractor expenditure is subject to restrictions for accounting periods beginning on or after 1 April 2024, although statutory exceptions may apply.

The location and nature of the activities, the necessity for overseas research and whether the relevant conditions could reasonably be replicated in the UK are important considerations.

Does grant funding prevent us from claiming R&D tax relief?

Under the new merged scheme (applies to accounting periods beginning on or after 1 April 2024), receiving a grant does not in itself disqualify expenditure from relief. However, contractual arrangements, who undertakes or initiates the R&D and other eligibility conditions must still be considered.

Can unsuccessful drug discovery or research projects qualify?

Yes. Qualifying R&D does not have to result in a successful scientific or commercial outcome.

Where activities seek to resolve genuine scientific or technological uncertainty, expenditure may qualify even if the research does not achieve the intended outcome.

Can loss-making biotechnology companies receive R&D tax support?

Yes.  Loss-making R&D-intensive SMEs may qualify for Enhanced R&D Intensive Support (ERIS), subject to the applicable intensity threshold and other conditions.

Other eligible companies may claim under the merged R&D expenditure credit scheme.

The appropriate scheme and benefit should be assessed using the company's financial circumstances.

What documentation should we maintain?

Relevant records may include research plans, laboratory reports, protocols, experimental results, trial records, scientific correspondence, contractual agreements and cost analyses. Together, these records should show the scientific or technological advance sought, the uncertainties encountered, the work undertaken and the connection between that work and the expenditure claimed.

Do we need to notify HMRC before making a claim?

Some companies must submit a claim notification within six months of the end of the relevant accounting period. Whether notification is required depends on the company’s recent claim history and the timing of earlier claims, so this should be checked well before the corporation tax return is filed.

Who can claim when a sponsor commissions work from a CRO or CDMO?

For accounting periods beginning on or after 1 April 2024, entitlement generally depends on which party intended or contemplated the relevant R&D when the contract was entered into. The contract is important, but HMRC may also consider the wider facts, including technical control, financial risk, IP arrangements and the parties’ understanding of the work.

How much R&D tax relief could we receive—and when?

The amount and timing will depend on the company’s accounting period, tax position, qualifying expenditure and the scheme under which it claims. For accounting periods beginning on or after 1 April 2024, most companies claim under the merged R&D expenditure credit scheme. This provides a taxable credit equal to 20% of qualifying R&D expenditure, giving a net benefit of 15% where the 25% main rate of corporation tax applies and 16.2% where the 19% small profits rate applies. The credit is first applied against the company’s corporation tax liability and then passes through a series of statutory payment steps. Any remaining amount may be payable in cash, subject to restrictions including the PAYE and NIC cap.

Loss-making SMEs that meet the R&D intensity threshold and the other qualifying conditions may instead claim Enhanced R&D Intensive Support (ERIS). For accounting periods beginning on or after 1 April 2024, the company’s qualifying R&D expenditure must generally equal at least 30% of its total relevant expenditure, although a one-year grace period may apply where the company qualified in the preceding period. ERIS provides an additional deduction equal to 86% of qualifying R&D expenditure. The enhanced loss may be surrendered for a payable tax credit at a rate of 14.5%, producing a maximum cash benefit of 26.97% of qualifying expenditure where the company has sufficient surrenderable losses. The actual benefit may be lower where its available losses restrict the amount that can be surrendered.

Payment timing is not fixed. It depends on when the accounts and corporation tax return are finalised and submitted, whether a claim notification and Additional Information Form have been completed correctly, HMRC’s processing time and whether the claim is selected for review. Companies should therefore avoid treating the estimated benefit as guaranteed cash on a particular date. Early modelling should establish a realistic range rather than a single headline figure, taking account of qualifying expenditure, the applicable scheme, grants, overseas costs, connected companies, the PAYE and NIC cap and available losses. This can support more reliable cash-flow forecasts, fundraising plans and board reporting.
