In short

What engineering and manufacturing work qualifies for R&D tax relief?

Work that seeks an advance in the wider field of science or technology and involves uncertainty a competent professional could not readily resolve. Technical difficulty, bespoke design or commercial improvement alone does not qualify.

What qualifies

Manufacturing R&D tax relief: what counts as qualifying R&D?

  • The key question is not whether work was innovative or commercially valuable, but whether it sought an advance in science or technology
  • Qualifying activities are often embedded within production and engineering operations
  • Technical difficulty, bespoke design and innovation do not automatically make work eligible
  • We look at the actual development work, not generic classifications of manufacturing spend

May qualify

  • Prototype work that directly contributes to resolving qualifying uncertainty
  • Modelling and simulation used to investigate a qualifying uncertainty
  • Process improvements seeking an advance in technological capability
  • Research on an operational production line, if directly resolving uncertainty
  • Unsuccessful projects and trials
  • Further R&D where new uncertainties emerge after a prototype

Assessed project by project.

Not ordinarily qualifying

  • Routine product changes and production optimisation
  • Adopting established manufacturing processes
  • Routine design calculations or verification using established methods
  • Commercial prototypes, demonstrations and validation after the uncertainty is resolved
  • The capital cost of buying machinery, by itself

Trials do not qualify merely because the outcome was uncertain.

Where R&D arises

Engineering and manufacturing R&D tax relief: where qualifying work arises

Short answer: where engineers seek an advance in technological capability and must resolve uncertainty that is not readily deducible, across engineering disciplines and manufacturing activity.

Engineering

  • Mechanical: new mechanical systems, components or performance capabilities
  • Electrical and electronic: electronic systems, power management, controls and integration
  • Civil and structural: new engineering methods, materials or structural technologies
  • Environmental: water treatment, waste processing, pollution management, resource recovery
  • Materials: new material properties and technical performance
  • Product development: experimental design, prototyping and technical validation
  • Systems: complex interactions between components or processes

Manufacturing

  • New product development: products with new or appreciably improved capabilities
  • Advanced materials: material properties, formulations, composition and performance
  • Manufacturing processes: new or enhanced methods to overcome limitations in production
  • Machinery and equipment: specialist systems, machinery and production equipment
  • Prototypes and experimental production: design, testing and technical evaluation to resolve uncertainty
  • Waste and resource recovery: new methods for recycling, processing and reusing materials

Not every part of an engineering or manufacturing project qualifies; each is assessed against the advance sought. Eligibility guide

Common challenges

Manufacturing R&D tax credits and more: seven common challenges

Short answer: telling difficulty from uncertainty, finding where R&D started and stopped, and separating experimental work and costs from routine production.

Our projects are technically difficult: does that mean they qualify?

The challenge
  • Complex client requirements, difficult specifications and demanding operating conditions are common
  • A project can need substantial expertise without advancing the wider field of technology
How iTax Advisors helps
  • Establish the technological baseline: what was publicly available or readily deducible and where established methods were inadequate
  • Identify the specific advance sought and the uncertainties
  • Work with competent professionals to separate qualifying uncertainty from routine design and implementation. Eligibility guide

When does engineering R&D begin and end?

The challenge
  • Projects move through feasibility, design, simulation, prototyping, testing, validation and production, and not all of it qualifies
  • It is hard to pinpoint when qualifying R&D began and the uncertainty was resolved, leading to over- or under-claiming
How iTax Advisors helps
  • Interview technical leads to reconstruct the phases and the “R&D clock”
  • Examine timelines, design revisions, calculations, simulations, prototype and test records, failures and key decisions
  • Separate qualifying work from scoping, routine design, commercial production and post-resolution validation

Can we claim the costs of prototypes and testing?

The challenge
  • Prototype work involves engineers, materials, specialist testing, software, contractors and capital equipment
  • Separating qualifying development from capital investment, routine verification and production is complex
How iTax Advisors helps
  • Review the purpose of the prototype work, the uncertainties and the nature of the spend
  • Identify potentially qualifying cost categories and build a documented financial analysis. Qualifying costs guide

We run production trials: are these R&D?

The challenge
  • Trials with new raw materials, processing parameters, machinery configurations and methods are common
  • A trial does not qualify merely because the outcome was uncertain; normal production experimentation is different
How iTax Advisors helps
  • Review the technological objectives, existing capabilities and technical issues
  • Examine trial records, test results and development decisions to see whether the work sought an advance competent professionals could not readily achieve

Our R&D is embedded within everyday operations

The challenge
  • Production engineers, technical managers and process specialists may do qualifying work as part of wider roles
  • It may not be recorded separately, so it is overlooked or hard to separate from routine operations
How iTax Advisors helps
  • Hold structured discussions with technical and operational staff across the business
  • Review technical developments beyond formal research departments to establish the scope and the people involved

How do we identify eligible materials and consumables?

The challenge
  • Experimental production and testing can use substantial raw materials, formulations, components and prototypes
  • Not all materials qualify, especially where activities overlap with commercial production
How iTax Advisors helps
  • Examine materials usage, project records and financial information
  • Build an evidence-based calculation separating experimental work from ordinary production

We improved an existing process: is that enough?

The challenge
  • Better yield, quality, consistency, waste levels or efficiency do not determine eligibility
  • Improvements using established techniques may not be qualifying R&D
How iTax Advisors helps
  • Consider whether the work sought a genuine technological advance beyond routine engineering or process optimisation
  • Document the technical baseline, limitations, experimental work and results

Machinery, subcontractors and overseas costs

  • The capital cost of machinery does not qualify by itself, but staff, consumables, software and contractor costs of developing or testing it may
  • R&D Allowances may be relevant for qualifying capital spend on R&D facilities or equipment
  • Subcontracted R&D depends on who intended or contemplated the R&D. Contracted-out R&D rules

Staff and materials

  • Production engineers’ salaries qualify to the extent they relate to qualifying R&D, on a reasonable, supportable allocation
  • Materials, components, water, fuel and power may qualify where consumed or transformed in R&D
  • Where R&D produces an item sold at arm’s length, the consumables in it cannot be claimed

FAQs

Manufacturing and engineering R&D tax relief questions

Short answers first. Speak to us about your own position.

Unsure whether your project qualifies?Ask a specialist
Does bespoke engineering design qualify for R&D tax relief?

Not automatically. A bespoke solution may involve considerable expertise without advancing overall technological knowledge. Eligibility depends on whether the work seeks an advance and involves qualifying uncertainty.

Can prototype development qualify?

Yes, where the prototype work directly contributes to resolving qualifying uncertainty. Routine demonstrations, commercial prototypes and ordinary validation after the uncertainty is resolved do not automatically qualify.

Can CAD modelling and engineering simulations qualify?

Potentially, where used to investigate qualifying technological uncertainty. Routine design calculations or verification using established methods would not ordinarily qualify.

Does improving an existing manufacturing process qualify?

Potentially, where it involves seeking an advance in technological capability and resolving uncertainty competent professionals could not readily overcome. Routine improvements using established techniques would not normally qualify.

Can unsuccessful production trials or engineering projects qualify?

Yes. Work undertaken to resolve qualifying uncertainty can be R&D even where the experiments fail or the intended improvement is not achieved.

Does purchasing new machinery qualify?

The capital cost does not qualify by itself under the revenue-based schemes. Staff, consumables, software, contractor and other revenue costs of developing, adapting or testing machinery may qualify, and R&D Allowances may be relevant for qualifying capital spend.

Can overseas engineering, testing or subcontractor costs qualify?

Overseas contractor and externally provided worker costs are generally restricted for accounting periods beginning on or after 1 April 2024. A narrow exception may apply, but lower cost or worker availability is not sufficient on its own.

Which R&D tax relief scheme applies to manufacturers?

For accounting periods beginning on or after 1 April 2024, most companies claim under the merged RDEC scheme. A separate enhanced scheme may be available to loss-making, R&D-intensive SMEs. Schemes guide
