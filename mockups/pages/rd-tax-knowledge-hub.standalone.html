Schemes and rates

How does R&D tax relief work? R&D tax credits explained

  • Open to companies across software, life sciences, manufacturing, engineering, construction and more
  • For accounting periods beginning on or after 1 April 2024, two forms of support are available
  • The merged RDEC scheme applies to qualifying companies of any size
  • ERIS supports certain loss-making, R&D-intensive SMEs

Who can claim R&D tax relief?

  • UK companies undertaking eligible R&D, subject to UK Corporation Tax
  • Expenditure is revenue in nature and tax-deductible in the UK
  • The claim relates to the company’s trade and the company is a going concern when it claims

Eligibility

R&D tax relief eligibility: does your project qualify?

Short answer: it qualifies if it seeks an advance in a field of science or technology and resolves uncertainty that a competent professional could not readily resolve using publicly available knowledge. Not all innovation qualifies.

Scientific or technological advance

  • An advance in overall knowledge or capability in a field, not just an internal improvement
  • It does not need to be new to the world: appreciable improvements to existing products, processes, services, materials or systems can qualify

Scientific or technological uncertainty

  • Competent professionals could not readily determine how to achieve the advance
  • It is a genuine technical challenge, not a commercial, financial or aesthetic one

Likely to qualify

  • Appreciably improving an existing product, process, service, material or device
  • More efficient or scalable technical solutions
  • Automating manual processes where it needs overcoming technological challenges
  • Improving system capability, reliability or performance
  • A fundamentally different technological approach to an existing outcome

Mathematical advances are also recognised as science from 1 April 2023.

Unlikely to qualify

  • Straightforward application of publicly available knowledge
  • Routine software configuration or standard data migration
  • Cosmetic or aesthetic changes
  • Market research or commercial innovation alone
  • Ordinary business process improvements without technological challenges

Routine debugging does not generally qualify.

R&D tax relief for beginners: four common misconceptions

“We didn’t invent something completely new.”

You do not need a groundbreaking invention. Improving existing systems can qualify.

“Our project failed.”

Projects can still qualify. Genuine R&D involves experimentation and uncertainty.

“A competitor already does something similar.”

If the methodology is not publicly available, your own attempts may be eligible.

“We only automated a manual process.”

It can qualify if it needed overcoming technological challenges.

Technical oversight

Who is a competent professional in an R&D tax claim?

  • HMRC guidance describes someone with the knowledge, experience and technical expertise to understand the uncertainties in a project
  • There is no formal definition in the legislation itself
  • They distinguish genuine uncertainty from work that is complex or costly but relies on established practice

They should be able to assess

  • Whether an advance in science or technology was being sought
  • The uncertainties met during development
  • Why the solution was not readily deducible
  • The work undertaken to resolve them

Who can fulfil the role?

Senior engineersSoftware architects or lead developersScientists or laboratory specialistsTechnical directorsProduct development managersExperienced industry specialistsExternal technical consultants
  • A formal academic qualification is not essential, though qualifications can support credibility
  • External consultants can act, but must genuinely understand the project work

Evidence of expertise

Years of industry experienceTechnical certificationsAcademic qualificationsChartered statusPublications or patentsProject leadershipSpecialist training

Qualifying costs

R&D qualifying costs: what can you claim?

Short answer: seven categories of expenditure directly linked to qualifying R&D. Only specific costs qualify, and mixed-use costs need a reasonable apportionment.

01

Staff costs

Gross salaries and wages, employer’s NIC and pension contributions. Only the R&D proportion of time qualifies.

02

Externally provided workers

Supplied by a staff provider and working under your supervision, direction or control. Typically restricted to 65% for unconnected providers.

03

Contracted-out R&D

The customer who intended or contemplated the R&D usually claims. Payments to unconnected contractors are restricted to 65%.

04

Consumables

Items used up or transformed in R&D, including water, fuel and power. Not items sold at arm’s length.

05

Software

Development environments, simulation, CAD and testing tools. Apportion partly used software; general admin software does not qualify.

06

Data licences and cloud

Hosting, compute, virtual machines, storage and datasets used directly in R&D. Separate these from production hosting.

07

Clinical trial participants

Payments made directly to participants. Payments routed through third parties such as the NHS or universities do not qualify.

Evidence HMRC expects for staff time

TimesheetsSprint recordsJira or DevOps logsProject plansTechnical meeting notesManagerial assessments

Overseas restrictions

  • Overseas EPW and subcontractor costs generally do not qualify
  • Exceptions: environmental, population-specific or legal and regulatory conditions, evidenced contemporaneously
  • No restriction on non-UK R&D by your own staff

The claim process

How to claim R&D tax relief: three steps

Short answer: check whether you must notify HMRC, submit the Additional Information Form, then make the claim in the CT600. The deadline is generally two years from the end of the accounting period.

1Check claim notification
  • For accounting periods beginning on or after 1 April 2023
  • Needed in some cases, including a first R&D claim or where your last claim was more than three years before the end of the current notification period
  • Notification period: first day of the period to six months after it ends
  • Failure to notify where required may mean the claim is rejected
2Submit the Additional Information Form
  • Mandatory for all R&D claims, before the CT600 is filed
  • Covers company details, expenditure breakdown, projects and the advance sought
  • HMRC rejects claims where it has not been submitted
3Make the claim in the CT600
  • Submit within the Company Tax Return once steps 1 and 2 are done
  • Accurate, robust and supported by technical and financial evidence to reduce enquiry risk. Claim review

What is R&D tax relief?

A UK government incentive designed to reward companies investing in innovation. Eligible businesses can reduce their Corporation Tax liability, claim a cash repayment where losses arise and recover a proportion of qualifying R&D expenditure.

What does HMRC look for in an R&D claim?

Clear, robust evidence that qualifying R&D took place and that the claim was prepared correctly. HMRC focuses on involvement of competent professionals, understanding of the legislation, a clear technical narrative in the Additional Information Form and accurate financial calculations.

Is R&D tax relief only available for new product development?

No. Appreciably improving an existing product, process, service, material or device can qualify, as can a fundamentally different technological approach to an existing outcome. Read the eligibility guide.

Can small businesses claim R&D tax relief?

Yes. Many SMEs are eligible, including companies that are pre-revenue, loss-making, early-stage or venture-backed. SME status is assessed at group level, so partner and linked enterprises may need to be considered.

What is an R&D intensive company?

An SME whose qualifying R&D expenditure is a significant proportion of its total relevant expenditure, generally at least 30% for the enhanced support. It may then qualify for ERIS, with a payable credit rate of 14.5%.

Can directors’ salaries be included in an R&D claim?

Yes. Directors actively involved in qualifying R&D can include the relevant proportion of staffing costs. Dividends are non-qualifying.

Can grant-funded expenditure be claimed?

Yes. For accounting periods beginning on or after 1 April 2024, companies can generally claim relief on subsidised or grant-funded expenditure under the merged scheme.

Is there a cap on payable R&D credits?

Yes. The payable credit is generally capped at £20,000 plus 300% of the company’s PAYE and NIC liabilities for the period. See all R&D tax relief FAQs.
