Your HMRC letter

HMRC R&D enquiry letter response: which letter have you received?

Not every letter from HMRC about an R&D claim is a formal enquiry, and the right response depends on which one you have. Check the heading and the legislation the letter refers to. If you cannot tell, ask the officer named on the letter to confirm.

Compliance check

Formal enquiry into the return

What it usually means

HMRC has opened a check into the return that contains the R&D claim. It ends with a closure notice.

Is a reply required?

The enquiry stays open until it is closed, so ignoring it does not make it go away.

What to do first

Work through the first 48 hours steps below and diary the deadline.

Often the start of an enquiry

Payable credit held while HMRC checks the claim

What it usually means

HMRC wants to review the claim before it pays out an R&D tax credit. This is often the start of an enquiry.

Is a reply required?

No payment is made until HMRC is satisfied, so a slow reply delays your cash.

What to do first

Ask the officer exactly what they need and send it promptly.

Informal

Informal request for information or documents

What it usually means

The officer is asking for explanations or records by letter, usually alongside an enquiry.

Is a reply required?

Not on its own, but HMRC can follow up with a formal notice if it does not get what it needs.

What to do first

Reply by the date given or agree an extension in writing.

Legal requirement

Formal information notice (Schedule 36, Finance Act 2008)

What it usually means

HMRC is legally requiring specific information or documents.

Is a reply required?

Yes. Penalties can apply for not complying, and some notices can be appealed.

What to do first

Take advice quickly and check the deadline and any appeal rights.

Not an enquiry

“Nudge” or one-to-many letter

What it usually means

HMRC is asking you to check your claim yourself. It is not an enquiry.

Is a reply required?

Usually no, but HMRC may open an enquiry later if it still has concerns.

What to do first

Review the claim honestly and correct any errors you find.

How far back can HMRC look?HMRC can usually open an enquiry within 12 months of the date the corporation tax return was filed, as long as it was filed on time. Once that window has closed, HMRC needs to raise a discovery assessment, normally limited to 4 years from the end of the accounting period, extended to 6 years where an inaccuracy was careless and 20 years where it was deliberate.
An enquiry is not a final decision that the claim is wrong.HMRC will consider the explanation and evidence before reaching an outcome.
The first few days

HMRC R&D compliance check: what to do in the first 48 hours

A calm, organised approach will help you protect deadlines, preserve evidence and prepare a more credible response. The first 48 hours are a suggested organising window, not an HMRC deadline. Follow the dates in your correspondence.

  1. 1

    Create a control sheet

    Record the deadline, accounting period, projects in scope, HMRC contact, internal owner and location of the supporting records.

  2. 2

    Preserve the evidence

    Secure relevant emails, project files, timesheets, payroll data, invoices and claim workings. Do not overwrite originals.

  3. 3

    Nominate one coordinator

    One person coordinates contact with HMRC so technical, finance and adviser responses do not diverge.

  4. 4

    Reconcile the claim as filed

    Obtain the final corporation tax return, computations, additional information form, technical narrative and cost schedule actually filed, not an earlier draft.

  5. 5

    Check the scope of the request

    Identify the questions, documents, accounting periods and projects covered. Seek advice if HMRC later issues a formal information notice.

  6. 6

    Keep your other deadlines

    Keep filing returns and paying other taxes by their normal deadlines. If an adviser deals with HMRC, check that authority is in place.

What is the HMRC R&D enquiry process?

An enquiry usually begins with a letter, so first identify which type you have: a formal enquiry (compliance check) into the return, a payable credit held while HMRC checks the claim, an informal request for information or documents, a formal information notice (Schedule 36, Finance Act 2008) or a “nudge” letter, which is not an enquiry. A formal enquiry ends with a closure notice. In the first days, create a control sheet, preserve the evidence and nominate one coordinator. We then review the submitted claim, supporting reports and HMRC correspondence, assess the technical basis and expenditure treatment, identify evidential gaps, prepare clear responses for HMRC and support communication through to conclusion of the enquiry.

What does HMRC usually ask about?

HMRC commonly questions whether the work meets the definition of R&D for tax purposes, whether genuine technological uncertainties existed, whether the projects sought an advance in science or technology, and whether the qualifying expenditure can be properly supported. Enquiry letters can often be detailed and request substantial technical and financial information relating to the submitted claim.

How do I respond to an HMRC R&D enquiry?

Handle it carefully and respond thoroughly, with clear explanations and supporting evidence that directly address HMRC’s concerns. Start by organising the response: preserve the evidence, nominate one coordinator and reconcile the claim as filed.

Can HMRC reject an entire claim?

Yes. If HMRC believes the projects or expenditure do not meet the legislative requirements, part or all of a claim may be denied. A clear and technically supported response can materially improve the quality of the defence.

How far back can HMRC open an R&D enquiry?

HMRC can usually open an enquiry within 12 months of the date the corporation tax return was filed, as long as it was filed on time. Once that window has closed, HMRC needs to raise a discovery assessment, normally limited to 4 years from the end of the accounting period, extended to 6 years where an inaccuracy was careless and 20 years where it was deliberate.

Do I have to reply to a “nudge” letter?

A nudge or one-to-many letter asks you to check your claim yourself. It is not an enquiry, so a reply is usually not required, but HMRC may open an enquiry later if it still has concerns. Review the claim honestly and correct any errors you find.
