Before you claim

Three steps every R&D claim must complete

The claim is submitted in the company’s Corporation Tax Return (CT600), but two steps come first.

1

Check whether you need to notify HMRC

Applies to accounting periods beginning on or after 1 April 2023. You must notify if:

  • You are claiming for the first time
  • Your last claim was made more than three years before the end of the current notification period
  • You claimed for the previous period in an amended return received on or after 1 April 2023, or HMRC removed the claim
The claim notification period starts on the first day of the period of account and ends six months after the period of account ends.
Update Claim notification is only relevant for claims filed before 1 April 2027. Source: iTax Advisors claim readiness checklist, HMRC guidance checked 5 November 2026.
2

Submit the Additional Information Form

Mandatory for all R&D claims, and submitted before the CT600. HMRC will reject claims where it has not been. If both are sent on the same day, submit the additional information form first, then the Company Tax Return.

  • Company information
  • Breakdown of qualifying expenditure
  • Details of the projects claimed
  • The scientific or technological advances sought
  • Contact details for the claim
3

Submit the claim in the CT600

Once notification and the AIF are done, submit the claim in the Corporation Tax Return.

  • Accurate
  • Robust
  • Supported by sufficient technical and financial evidence

Is your claim ready?

The 15-point claim readiness check

Fifteen checks across five areas to review the technical, financial and procedural readiness of your R&D claim before you submit. It is a readiness check, not an eligibility score.

1

Eligibility and applicable scheme

Assess the company, accounting period and applicable relief before preparing the claim.

  1. 1Have you confirmed the company’s eligibility, accounting period and applicable R&D scheme, including any scheme-specific conditions?
  2. 2Does each project clearly explain the scientific or technological advance sought against the existing knowledge or capability in the field?
  3. 3Are the uncertainties explained, including why their resolution was not readily available or deducible by a competent professional?
2

Project scope and technical review

Explain the work in enough detail to support the claim.

  1. 4Is a relevant competent professional identified for each project, with their qualifications, experience and reasoned technical assessment recorded?
  2. 5Are the qualifying R&D start and end points identified, with routine, commercial and other non-qualifying activities distinguished?
  3. 6Does the project account describe the approach, tests, iterations and results, including unresolved uncertainties or unsuccessful work?
3

Qualifying costs and activities

Link expenditure to qualifying activities and apply the rules for the period and scheme.

  1. 7Do costs map to qualifying activities, including separately identified qualifying indirect activities where applicable?
  2. 8Are expenditure categories, staff-time allocations, other costs apportionments and estimates supported, with non-qualifying amounts and duplicates excluded?
  3. 9Do claim workings reconcile to the accounting records, with applicable rates, limits and PAYE cap or exemption checked?
4

Contracts, contracted-out expenditure and overseas costs

Review contractual entitlement separately from the costs claimed.

  1. 10Have contracts and surrounding circumstances been reviewed to establish who is entitled to claim for R&D contracted out by or to the company?
  2. 11Are contractor and externally provided worker costs separately identified, apportioned to qualifying activities and calculated under the applicable scheme rules?
  3. 12Have the locations of contractor and externally provided worker activities been recorded, with overseas cost restrictions checked and any exception supported by evidence?
5

Evidence, deadlines and submission

Keep a retrievable evidence trail and check procedural requirements as well as eligibility.

  1. 13Can you produce dated project and financial records supporting the work, its timing and costs, with retrospective explanations clearly identified?
  2. 14Have claim submission and claim notification (relevant only until 31 March 2027) deadlines been checked, or a documented exception established?
  3. 15Is the additional information form complete and consistent with the claim, with submission before the CT600 arranged and company approval and final filing checks completed?

Yes. Support can be provided in responding to HMRC questions or enquiries relating to prepared claims, including assistance with technical explanations and supporting documentation. Read about HMRC enquiry support.

What are the benefits of using specialist R&D tax advisers?

A specialist-led approach brings a deeper level of technical challenge, scrutiny and judgement. Claims are assessed in detail against current HMRC expectations, with a focus on technical credibility, evidencing and defensibility, which helps improve stakeholder confidence and reduce overall tax risk.

What must we submit to make an R&D tax relief claim?

Every claim requires an Additional Information Form before, or on the same day as, the Company Tax Return is submitted. Some companies must also submit a claim notification form within the applicable time limit. Eligibility for the underlying R&D does not remove these procedural requirements, so the filing position should be checked early.
