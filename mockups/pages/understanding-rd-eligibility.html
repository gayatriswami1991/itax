In short

What qualifies as R&D for UK tax purposes?

A project qualifies if it seeks an advance in science or technology and involves overcoming scientific or technological uncertainty related to that advance. “New” or “complex” on its own is not enough.

The definition

What qualifies as R&D for UK tax purposes?

  • The definition is intentionally broad, but applying it needs careful assessment of the work
  • A project does not need to be new to the world
  • The key question: did the work resolve challenges a competent professional could not easily solve using publicly available knowledge or standard industry techniques?
  • Mathematical advances are recognised as science from 1 April 2023

Activities that may still qualify

  • Appreciably improving an existing product, process, service, material or device
  • Developing more efficient or scalable technical solutions
  • Automating previously manual processes
  • Improving system capability, reliability or performance
  • Achieving an existing outcome through a fundamentally different technological approach
Core criteria

R&D tax relief eligibility criteria: advance and uncertainty

Short answer: both must be present. The advance is what you sought; the uncertainty is why it was not readily achievable.

1. Scientific or technological advance

  • Advances overall knowledge or capability in a field, not just an internal improvement
  • May be a new product, service, process or system, or an appreciable improvement to an existing one
  • The outcome was not readily achievable using existing knowledge or standard practice

2. Scientific or technological uncertainty

  • Competent professionals could not readily determine how to achieve the advance
  • It is a genuine technical challenge, not a commercial, financial or aesthetic one
  • Routine debugging, cosmetic changes or standard techniques generally do not qualify

Uncertainty typically exists where

  • Existing knowledge does not give a clear or reliable solution
  • Multiple approaches must be evaluated through experimentation
  • The outcome cannot be predicted in advance with confidence
Qualifying or not

R&D eligible activities: what is unlikely to qualify?

Unlikely to qualify

  • Straightforward application of publicly available knowledge
  • Routine software configuration
  • Standard data migration
  • Cosmetic or aesthetic changes
  • Market research or commercial innovation alone
  • Ordinary business process improvements without technological challenges

The distinguishing question

Did the work require overcoming scientific or technological uncertainty?

If a competent professional could reasonably determine how to achieve the outcome with current knowledge, tools and techniques, the work is unlikely to qualify, even if time-consuming.

If it could not be achieved without experimentation, iteration or new approaches, it is more likely to fall within the definition.

Common trap

Why “new” or “complex” isn’t enough for R&D tax relief

!“New” is not sufficient
  • New to a business, team or product does not in itself indicate R&D
  • First-time implementation, unfamiliar tools or new features can still rely on established knowledge
  • The test: does the work extend what is known or achievable in the wider field?
!“Complex” is not sufficient
  • Large, intricate, resource-intensive projects can still be achievable with existing techniques
  • The test is not how hard it is to deliver, but whether there is genuine uncertainty in how it can be achieved

Common pitfalls in R&D claims

  • Equating product innovation with technological advancement
  • Confusing difficulty with uncertainty
  • Overlooking the real R&D, often embedded in problem-solving and experimentation

Misconceptions

R&D tax relief for beginners: four common misconceptions

Short answer: many businesses assume their work is “normal day-to-day problem solving”. Eligible R&D often sits inside commercial projects and operational development.

“We didn’t invent something completely new.”

You do not need groundbreaking inventions. Improving existing systems or a new technological approach may qualify.

“Our project failed.”

Projects can still qualify. HMRC recognises that genuine R&D involves experimentation and uncertainty.

“A competitor already does something similar.”

If the underlying technology or methodology is not publicly available, your attempts may be eligible.

“We only automated a manual process.”

Automation can qualify if it needed overcoming technological challenges.

R&D tax relief examples by sector

Software, IT and technology

Advance sought
  • Developing AI-powered platforms that process high-volume real-time data securely and efficiently
  • Machine learning models for fraud detection or predictive analytics beyond existing industry capability

Related uncertainty
  • Whether the architecture could maintain performance, scalability and low latency under large concurrent workloads without compromising security

FAQs

R&D eligibility questions answered

Short answers first. Speak to us about your own project.

Not sure your project qualifies?Ask a specialist
Is R&D tax relief only for entirely new products?

No. Appreciably improving an existing product, process, service, material or device can qualify, as can achieving an existing outcome through a fundamentally different technological approach.

Can a project that failed still qualify?

Yes. HMRC recognises that genuine R&D involves experimentation and uncertainty, so an unsuccessful outcome does not rule a project out.

Does automating a manual process qualify?

It can, where achieving the outcome required overcoming technological challenges or developing a fundamentally different technical approach.

Do routine debugging or cosmetic changes qualify?

Generally not. Routine debugging, cosmetic changes and applying standard techniques do not normally qualify.

Do I need a competent professional to assess eligibility?

HMRC expects claims to be supported by individuals with relevant scientific or technological expertise who understand the uncertainties faced and the advances sought. Who is a competent professional?

What if a competitor already does something similar?

If the underlying technology or methodology is not publicly available, your own attempts to solve the challenge may still be eligible.
