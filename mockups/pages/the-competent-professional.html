Who Is a Competent Professional in an R&D Tax Claim?

By Aarti Varia · Updated 5 November 2026 · Knowledge Hub

Quick answer: what is a competent professional?

A person with the knowledge, experience and technical expertise to understand the scientific or technological uncertainties in a project. HMRC expects R&D claims to be supported by such individuals, who may be internal staff or external specialists.

Who is a competent professional?

Short answer: someone with the knowledge, experience and technical expertise to understand the scientific or technological uncertainties in a project.

There is no formal definition of a competent professional in the R&D tax legislation itself, but HMRC guidance describes one in these terms. In practice, they typically have:

  • a strong technical foundation in the relevant scientific or technological field;
  • practical experience dealing with similar challenges; and
  • a clear understanding of the current state of knowledge in their area.

Their involvement matters because they are positioned to distinguish genuine uncertainty, where the outcome could not readily be known in advance, from work that is complex, costly or highly specialised but relies on established principles, standard practice or readily available solutions.

What should a competent professional be able to assess?

  • whether an advance in science or technology was being sought;
  • the scientific or technological uncertainties encountered during development;
  • why the solution was not readily deducible by a competent professional working in the field; and
  • the work undertaken to attempt to resolve those uncertainties.

They therefore play a central role in supporting and validating that the activities claimed meet the legislative definition of R&D for tax purposes.

Who can be a competent professional?

Short answer: a formal academic qualification is not required, though qualifications can support credibility.

The role may be fulfilled by:

  • senior engineers
  • software architects or lead developers
  • scientists or laboratory specialists
  • technical directors
  • product development managers
  • experienced industry specialists
  • external technical consultants

The key requirement is relevant expertise in the specific field of the project. For example:

  • a software R&D project may rely on a senior software engineer or systems architect;
  • a manufacturing innovation project may need a mechanical or process engineer; and
  • a pharmaceutical development project may need oversight from a chemist or biomedical specialist.

What experience and evidence should they have?

HMRC generally expects the individual to demonstrate:

  • practical experience within the relevant technical field;
  • an understanding of current industry knowledge and limitations;
  • involvement in the project or technical review process;
  • the ability to explain why uncertainties existed; and
  • knowledge of the methods used to attempt resolution.

Relevant evidence may include years of industry experience, technical certifications, academic qualifications, chartered status, publications or patents, project leadership responsibilities and specialist technical training. Real-world technical expertise is often more important than formal qualifications alone.

Can external consultants act as competent professionals?

Short answer: yes, but they must have a genuine understanding of the project work.

Businesses may rely on external consultants or advisers with specialist expertise. HMRC generally expects the competent professional to understand the work undertaken, so external consultants should work closely with the internal technical team to assess the qualifying activities accurately.

Frequently asked questions

What is a competent professional in an R&D tax claim?

An individual with the knowledge, experience and technical expertise to understand the scientific or technological uncertainties in a project and to assess whether the work sought an advance.

Does a competent professional need formal qualifications?

No. A formal academic qualification is not essential, although qualifications can support credibility. Real-world technical expertise is often more important.

Can an external consultant be the competent professional?

Yes, if they have a genuine understanding of the project work and work closely with the internal technical team.

What does HMRC expect from competent professionals?

HMRC expects technical assessments to be made by individuals with relevant expertise and direct involvement in the R&D activities, who may be internal employees or external specialists.

How should a competent professional’s expertise be evidenced?

With evidence such as years of industry experience, technical certifications, academic qualifications, chartered status, publications or patents, project leadership responsibilities and specialist technical training.
