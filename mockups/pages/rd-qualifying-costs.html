R&D Qualifying Costs: What You Can Claim (Including Contracted-Out R&D)

By Aarti Varia · Updated 5 November 2026 · Knowledge Hub

Quick answer: what costs qualify for R&D tax relief?

Only specific categories of expenditure directly linked to qualifying R&D: staff costs, externally provided workers, contracted-out R&D, consumables, software, data licences and cloud computing, and payments to clinical trial participants. Mixed-use costs need a reasonable apportionment.

Staff costs

Short answer: for most businesses, staff costs are the largest part of an R&D claim, but only the proportion spent on R&D qualifies.

You can generally claim:

  • gross salaries and wages;
  • employer’s National Insurance contributions;
  • employer pension contributions;
  • certain bonuses linked to R&D work; and
  • reimbursed employee expenditure, to the extent it is incurred for R&D and relates to R&D cost categories.

Where employees split their time between qualifying and non-qualifying work, only the R&D proportion can be claimed. If a developer spends 70% of their time on eligible R&D projects, only 70% of their employment costs would normally qualify. HMRC expects time apportionments to be justified with evidence such as:

  • timesheets;
  • sprint records;
  • Jira or DevOps logs;
  • project plans;
  • technical meeting notes; and
  • managerial assessments.

Excluded: non-cash staff benefits, for example vouchers, car benefit (rather than a cash benefit for a car), redundancy payments and un-exercised share options.

Key points:

  • Individuals whose costs are claimed should have their employment contracts with the claimant company.
  • Only tax-deductible expenditure may be claimed, so consider the timing of pension and bonus payments for the claim period.
  • There is no restriction on claiming for non-UK R&D undertaken by your own staff.

Externally provided workers (EPWs)

Short answer: individuals supplied by a staff provider, working under your direction, qualify at 65% of the payment for unconnected providers and up to 100% for connected ones.

EPWs are individuals supplied by a staff provider, such as agency workers, contractors and freelancers engaged through a provider. To qualify:

  • the workers must operate under the claimant company’s supervision, direction or control (other conditions also apply);
  • the work must relate directly to eligible R&D; and
  • there must be a payment for “qualifying earnings” to a staff provider or staff controller. In most cases the EPW’s earnings must be subject to UK PAYE and National Insurance, although exceptions apply.

For unconnected EPWs, claims are typically restricted to 65% of the relevant payment. You can claim up to 100% of relevant payments for eligible R&D to a connected staff provider or controller, for example a group company or any other company where an election is made to be treated as connected.

Contracted-out R&D

Short answer: the customer who intended or contemplated the R&D usually claims, not the contractor doing the work.

  • The rules changed for accounting periods beginning on or after 1 April 2024.
  • R&D is contracted out where it is reasonable to assume the customer intended or contemplated that R&D would be undertaken. The arrangement may cover a whole commercial project, a specific R&D project or part of a wider one.
  • Commissioning a technically complex project is not enough. The customer must have understood and contemplated the particular R&D required.
  • Payments to unconnected contractors or subcontractors are restricted to 65%; where the parties are connected, the full qualifying amount is claimable.
  • Routine commercial services or standard goods do not become qualifying simply because they support an R&D project.

Consumables

Short answer: items used directly in R&D that are used up or transformed in the process.

  • Consumables such as raw materials, test materials or chemicals used in testing may be claimed. Qualifying expenditure also includes the cost of water, fuel and power.
  • The items must be used in the R&D and no longer be usable in their original form.
  • Where consumables, prototypes or first-in-class items are sold or ownership is transferred at arm’s length, the costs cannot be claimed. A prototype destroyed during testing may qualify; one sold at arm’s length will not.
  • Keep clear records linking consumable costs to specific R&D activities or experimental phases.

Software

Short answer: software used directly in qualifying R&D can often be included, apportioned where it is only partly used for R&D.

  • Eligible software may include development environments, simulation software, CAD tools, technical modelling platforms, testing tools and specialist analytics software.
  • A cloud development platform used 80% for experimental software development may be claimed at 80%.
  • General business software used mainly for administration would usually not qualify.

Data licences and cloud computing

Short answer: cloud, compute and data costs qualify when used directly for R&D, not for general commercial operations.

  • Qualifying expenditure can include cloud hosting used directly for R&D, data processing environments, compute infrastructure, virtual machines, data storage for R&D activities, AI and machine learning datasets and data licence fees.
  • This is especially important for SaaS businesses, AI and machine learning companies, software developers and data-heavy engineering businesses.
  • Cloud and data costs relating to general commercial operations or qualifying indirect activities must be excluded.
  • Separate experimental environments, testing infrastructure and R&D datasets from production hosting, customer-facing systems and general IT overhead.

Payments to clinical trial participants

Short answer: payments to trial volunteers can qualify, but only when paid directly to the participants.

  • Life sciences and pharmaceutical businesses may claim payments to clinical trial volunteers where the expenditure relates directly to qualifying R&D.
  • Where payments are routed through third parties such as the NHS or universities, they are non-qualifying.
  • This category is specialist and generally needs detailed supporting documentation.

Overseas restrictions and exemptions

Short answer: overseas EPW and subcontractor costs generally do not qualify, unless the activity must necessarily be undertaken outside the UK.

  • The exemptions cover environmental conditions, social or population-specific requirements and legal or regulatory requirements.
  • HMRC expects robust contemporaneous evidence of why the activity could not reasonably be carried out in the UK.
  • Keep technical specifications and project scopes, evidence of oversight and decision-making, correspondence showing responsibility for the R&D, financial records supporting cost allocations and evidence supporting any exemption.

What HMRC may ask to see

HMRC scrutiny of R&D claims has increased significantly in recent years. It may ask for supporting detail such as:

  • invoices;
  • financial reconciliations;
  • payroll evidence;
  • contractor agreements;
  • R&D cost allocation methodologies; and
  • clear cost schedules.

Qualifying costs: frequently asked questions

What costs qualify for R&D tax relief?

Staff costs, externally provided workers, contracted-out R&D, consumables (including water, fuel and power), software, data licences and cloud computing, and payments to clinical trial participants, where they are directly linked to qualifying R&D.

Can directors’ salaries be included in an R&D claim?

Yes. Directors actively involved in qualifying R&D activities may include the relevant proportion of staffing costs relating to R&D. Dividends are non-qualifying costs.

What if employees split their time between R&D and other work?

Only the R&D proportion can be claimed. For example, if a developer spends 70% of their time on eligible R&D projects, only 70% of their employment costs would normally qualify, supported by evidence such as timesheets, sprint records or Jira logs.

Which staff benefits are excluded?

Non-cash staff benefits must be excluded, for example vouchers, car benefit (rather than a cash benefit for a car), redundancy payments and un-exercised share options.

How much of external worker or contractor costs can be claimed?

For unconnected externally provided workers and unconnected contractors or subcontractors, claims are typically restricted to 65% of the relevant payment. For connected parties, up to 100% may be claimed.

Can I claim cloud computing and software costs?

Yes, where they are used directly in qualifying R&D. Cloud hosting, compute, virtual machines, storage, datasets and data licence fees can qualify, but costs relating to general commercial operations, production hosting and general IT overhead must be excluded, and partly used software should be apportioned.

Can I claim for R&D done overseas?

There is no restriction on claiming for non-UK R&D undertaken by your own staff. Overseas externally provided worker and subcontractor costs are restricted for accounting periods beginning on or after 1 April 2024, unless the conditions for the R&D are not present in the UK or legal, regulatory, environmental or population-specific factors make overseas activity unavoidable.

Can grant-funded expenditure be claimed?

Yes. For accounting periods beginning on or after 1 April 2024, companies can generally claim R&D tax relief on subsidised or grant-funded expenditure under the merged R&D scheme.
