Customer and contractor: whose R&D is it?
In short

Who can claim contracted-out R&D?

Generally the party that decided to initiate the R&D: the customer who intended or contemplated R&D of that sort when entering the contract, not necessarily the party whose staff did the work.

The new test

Who can claim contracted-out R&D? The rules from 1 April 2024

  • A significant change for accounting periods beginning on or after 1 April 2024
  • “Who actually carried out the R&D?” is no longer the right question
  • HMRC’s general principle: identify the party that decided to initiate the R&D
  • The contractor’s staff doing the work does not necessarily mean the contractor owns the claim

The key question

When entering the contract, did the customer intend or contemplate R&D of that sort?

It goes beyond knowing that some technical development may be needed.

Intended or contemplated

What does “intended or contemplated” R&D mean?

Short answer: a specific appreciation of what R&D will be done, and the ability to understand and specify it.

Not enough on its own

  • Recognising that a project will involve technical challenges
  • Engaging specialists to resolve them

HMRC says “intended or contemplated” requires more than this.

Contemplating R&D can be read as

  • Identifying technological challenges and uncertainties, without necessarily knowing how they may be resolved
  • Assessing alternative technical approaches
  • Concluding that existing knowledge may be insufficient
  • Deciding to commission the work because R&D is expected to be necessary
When entitlement moves

When contracted-out R&D shifts from customer to contractor

Short answer: a project can start as the customer’s R&D and later become the contractor’s, so the boundaries can move.

1The customer contemplates the R&D
  • A manufacturer develops a new product
  • It identifies a technological uncertainty and commissions a specialist engineering company to resolve it
  • The engineers do all the experimental work, but the claim sits with the manufacturer
2An unplanned problem appears
  • The engineering company meets a technological problem
  • The manufacturer neither identified nor planned for it
3The contractor decides to act
  • The engineering company independently decides to undertake R&D to resolve it
  • The engineering company may be the party entitled to claim for that work

Costs

R&D tax relief for subcontractors: what can you claim?

Short answer: 65% of payments to unconnected contractors, and the full amount where the parties are connected.

  • Payments to unconnected contractors or subcontractors: claim restricted to 65% of the qualifying payment
  • Where claimant and contractor are connected: the full qualifying amount is claimable
  • Routine commercial services or standard goods do not become qualifying R&D just because they support an R&D project

An important exception

  • Where the customer is not eligible to claim UK R&D relief, for example an overseas entity outside UK Corporation Tax

Overseas

Overseas subcontractor R&D tax relief: the restrictions

Short answer: generally not allowed, unless the activity must necessarily happen outside the UK.

  • Overseas restrictions apply to externally provided workers and contracted-out R&D
  • In most cases overseas costs will not qualify unless the activity must necessarily be undertaken outside the UK
  • HMRC expects robust contemporaneous evidence explaining why it could not reasonably be done in the UK

In practice

Contracted-out R&D in practice: ask “whose R&D was it?”

Short answer: entitlement depends on the technical project, the contractual context and, most of all, who contemplated and decided that R&D needed to be undertaken.

Not “Who did the work?”

“Whose R&D is this activity actually advancing?”

  • Applies to R&D through customers, consultants, software developers, engineering firms and other contractors
  • The written contract still matters, alongside the technical facts and the decision-making that led to the R&D

Evidence to retain

Contracted-out R&D questions answered

Who can claim contracted-out R&D?

Generally the party that decided to initiate the R&D. For accounting periods beginning on or after 1 April 2024, that is usually the customer who intended or contemplated R&D of that sort when entering the contract, not necessarily the party whose staff did the work.

What does “intended or contemplated” R&D mean?

It means a specific appreciation of the R&D to be done: identifying technological challenges and uncertainties, assessing alternative approaches, concluding that existing knowledge may be insufficient and deciding to commission the work because R&D is expected to be necessary. Simply recognising technical challenges and hiring specialists is not enough.

Can entitlement to claim change during a project?

Yes. A project may start as the customer’s R&D and later become the contractor’s, for example where the contractor meets a problem the customer neither identified nor planned for and independently decides to undertake R&D to resolve it.

Does the contractor own the claim because its engineers did the work?

Not necessarily. The fact that the contractor’s engineers, developers or scientists physically perform the work does not mean the contractor owns the R&D claim.

Is the written contract enough to decide who claims?

No. The contract remains important, but HMRC’s guidance says other surrounding circumstances must be considered, such as financial risk, autonomy, IP ownership and who contemplated the need for R&D.

Can a UK contractor claim if the customer is overseas?

Possibly. Where the customer is not eligible to claim UK R&D tax relief, for example an overseas entity outside the scope of UK Corporation Tax, the UK contractor may be able to claim relief for its own qualifying R&D expenditure, subject to the relevant conditions.

How much of a contractor payment can be claimed?

For payments to unconnected contractors or subcontractors, the claim is restricted to 65% of the qualifying payment. Where the parties are connected, the full amount of qualifying expenditure is claimable.

Do overseas subcontractor costs qualify?

In most cases they do not, unless the activity must necessarily be carried out outside the UK because of environmental conditions, social or population-specific requirements, or legal or regulatory requirements. Read the qualifying costs guide.
