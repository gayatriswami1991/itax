R&D Tax Relief Schemes: Merged RDEC and ERIS Explained

By Aarti Varia · Updated 5 November 2026 · Knowledge Hub

Quick answer: which R&D tax relief schemes are available?

For accounting periods beginning on or after 1 April 2024: the merged RDEC scheme (20% taxable credit, 15%–16.2% net benefit) for companies of any size, and ERIS (86% enhanced deduction, 14.5% payable credit) for certain loss-making, R&D-intensive SMEs.

Two forms of R&D tax relief from 1 April 2024

Short answer: the merged RDEC scheme applies to companies of any size; ERIS supports certain loss-making, R&D-intensive SMEs.

R&D tax relief is claimable by companies across a wide range of sectors, including software, life sciences, manufacturing, engineering and construction. For accounting periods beginning on or after 1 April 2024, two forms of support are available.

The merged RDEC scheme

Short answer: a 20% taxable credit that first offsets corporation tax.

  • Applies to companies carrying out qualifying R&D, regardless of size
  • A taxable above-the-line credit of 20% on qualifying R&D expenditure
  • Net benefit generally between 15% and 16.2%, depending on the company’s corporation tax rate
  • The credit can first be used to offset corporation tax liability
  • Subject to restrictions and caps, any excess is receivable in cash
  • Unused amounts may be carried forward or surrendered in certain group situations

Enhanced R&D Intensive Support (ERIS)

Short answer: an 86% enhanced deduction and a 14.5% payable credit for loss-making SMEs that are R&D intensive.

  • Available to certain loss-making SMEs that are R&D intensive
  • R&D intensive generally means qualifying R&D expenditure is at least 30% of total relevant expenditure for the accounting period
  • Qualifying R&D expenditure receives an enhanced deduction of 86%
  • Surrenderable losses attributable to R&D may qualify for a payable tax credit of 14.5%

When calculating the ratio, connected companies may need to be aggregated and certain tax adjustments apply to the figures.

What is an SME for R&D tax purposes?

Short answer: generally fewer than 500 employees, and either turnover below €100 million or gross assets below €86 million, assessed at group level.

  • Fewer than 500 employees, and
  • annual turnover below €100 million, or gross assets (balance sheet total) below €86 million

These thresholds are assessed at group level and may require consideration of partner enterprises (25%–50% ownership) and linked enterprises (over 50% ownership or control). This can significantly affect eligibility, particularly for venture-backed or group companies.

Who can claim R&D tax relief?

Short answer: UK companies undertaking eligible R&D.

  • The company must be subject to UK Corporation Tax
  • The claimed expenditure must be revenue in nature and deductible for tax in the UK
  • The claim must relate to the trade of the company
  • The company must be a going concern when the claim is made

Is there a cap on payable R&D credits?

Short answer: yes, generally £20,000 plus 300% of the company’s PAYE and NIC liabilities for the period.

A company may be exempt from the cap where:

  • employees are actively involved in creating or managing intellectual property (IP); and
  • less than 15% of qualifying R&D expenditure relates to connected-party subcontractors or externally provided workers (EPWs).

Careful analysis is often required to determine whether the exemption applies.

Frequently asked questions

What are the R&D tax relief schemes from 1 April 2024?

The merged R&D expenditure credit scheme (RDEC) for companies of any size, and Enhanced R&D Intensive Support (ERIS) for certain loss-making, R&D-intensive SMEs.

What is the RDEC rate?

A taxable above-the-line credit of 20% of qualifying R&D expenditure. The net benefit is generally between 15% and 16.2%, depending on the company’s corporation tax rate.

What is an R&D intensive company?

An SME whose qualifying R&D expenditure is generally at least 30% of its total relevant expenditure for the accounting period.

How much benefit can an R&D intensive company receive?

Eligible R&D intensive loss-making SMEs get an 86% enhanced deduction and may qualify for a payable credit rate of 14.5% on surrenderable losses.

Can small businesses claim R&D tax relief?

Yes. Many SMEs are eligible, including companies that are pre-revenue, loss-making, early-stage or venture-backed. SME status is assessed at group level.

Is there a cap on payable R&D credits?

Yes. The payable credit is generally capped at £20,000 plus 300% of PAYE and NIC liabilities, with an exemption in limited circumstances.

Can grant-funded expenditure be claimed?

Yes. For accounting periods beginning on or after 1 April 2024, companies can generally claim relief on subsidised or grant-funded expenditure under the merged scheme.
